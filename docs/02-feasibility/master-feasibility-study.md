# Master Feasibility Study
## Eastern Indonesia Digital Education Initiative

## 1. Purpose

This Feasibility Study (FS) is intended to determine whether the proposed
education initiative is viable, affordable, legally implementable,
operationally sustainable, and aligned with demonstrated demand.

It is a framework for research and decision-making. It is not a final
investment recommendation until the required evidence and calculations
have been completed.

## 2. Projects Under Review

### Workstream A — SMA IT Gowa
A proposed senior secondary school with a preliminary capacity target
of 540 students at maturity.

### Workstream B — Digital Skills Course Institution
A proposed training institution offering practical digital-skills
courses, subject to market validation and applicable requirements.

### Workstream C — Future University Pathway
A later-stage option that requires its own demand study, legal analysis,
academic plan, capital model, and regulatory review.

### Workstream D — Digital Infrastructure
Technology that may support the education workstreams, subject to
security, privacy, availability, and cost assessments.

### Workstream E — Data Center
A separate investment case with a preliminary planning envelope of
IDR 1.1 trillion. This figure is unverified and must not be treated as
an approved budget, financing commitment, or vendor quotation.

## 3. Feasibility Dimensions

The study should cover:

1. Market and demand feasibility.
2. Technical and site feasibility.
3. Legal and regulatory feasibility.
4. Organizational and operational feasibility.
5. Human-resource feasibility.
6. Financial and funding feasibility.
7. Environmental, social, and safety considerations.
8. Risk, resilience, and implementation feasibility.

## 4. Market Feasibility

Collect evidence on:
- Population and school-age demographics.
- Existing schools and alternative education providers.
- Tuition and course-price benchmarks.
- Family affordability and preferences.
- Student and parent survey results.
- Employer and industry skills requirements.
- Potential corporate and institutional training demand.
- Competitor locations, offerings, and differentiators.
- Willingness to enroll at proposed prices.

Use dated, attributable sources and record the method and sample size
for surveys. Do not present informal interest as confirmed enrollment.

## 5. Technical and Site Feasibility

For the school and course institution, assess:
- Site availability, title, zoning, access, and suitability.
- Space requirements and occupancy.
- Construction and fit-out requirements.
- Electricity, water, internet, and backup.
- Classroom and laboratory equipment.
- Safety, accessibility, and maintenance.
- IT architecture, privacy, and cybersecurity.

The data center requires a separate technical study covering power,
connectivity, cooling, resilience, site conditions, hardware, security,
and customer demand.

## 6. Legal and Regulatory Feasibility

Obtain written guidance from competent authorities and qualified
advisers on:
- Legal entity and ownership.
- School establishment and operating approvals.
- Curriculum and staffing requirements.
- Course institution and training authorization.
- Building, zoning, fire, health, and safety requirements.
- Data protection and cybersecurity obligations.
- Tax, employment, accounting, and reporting.
- Agreements with investors, donors, and partners.

The future university pathway must be evaluated independently under
the applicable higher-education rules.

## 7. Operational Feasibility

Prepare operating models for each workstream:
- Governance and management.
- Staffing and organization charts.
- Admissions, scheduling, and learner support.
- Academic delivery and quality assurance.
- Procurement and asset management.
- IT operations and incident management.
- Finance, internal controls, and reporting.
- Maintenance and business continuity.

## 8. Financial Feasibility

Build separate financial models for:
- SMA IT establishment and operations.
- Course institution establishment and operations.
- Future university pathway.
- Shared digital infrastructure.
- Data center investment case.

For each model, include:
- Initial capital expenditure (CAPEX).
- Annual operating expenditure (OPEX).
- Revenue assumptions and collection timing.
- Funding sources and conditions.
- Working capital and cash runway.
- Depreciation and replacement needs where relevant.
- Tax and other applicable costs.
- Contingency and sensitivity analysis.

Do not combine project cash flows merely to make one workstream
appear financially sustainable.

## 9. Required Financial Scenarios

At minimum, model:
- Conservative case.
- Base case supported by evidence.
- Upside case with clearly stated assumptions.

Test lower enrollment, slower growth, tuition pressure, cost escalation,
delayed opening, reduced sponsorship, and higher working-capital needs.

Report cash balances and funding gaps by period, not only headline
profitability.

## 10. Investment and Funding Assessment

For each source of funding, record:
- Provider and amount.
- Whether funds are proposed, committed, or received.
- Conditions and restrictions.
- Expected timing.
- Repayment, ownership, or return terms where applicable.
- Required approvals and supporting agreements.

Do not count unsigned investor interest as committed capital.

## 11. Risk Assessment

Assess demand, regulatory, construction, funding, staffing, operations,
cybersecurity, data privacy, scholarship continuity, and reputation risks.

Every material risk should have an owner, likelihood, impact, mitigation,
early-warning indicator, and review date.

## 12. Decision Framework

Recommended decision outcomes:
- Proceed to the next diligence stage.
- Proceed only after specified conditions are met.
- Redesign scope or financial assumptions.
- Defer pending evidence or approvals.
- Stop the workstream if viability cannot be demonstrated.

## 13. Deliverables

The completed FS should include:
1. Executive summary.
2. Market and demand report.
3. Site and technical report.
4. Legal and regulatory memorandum.
5. Operating and staffing plan.
6. CAPEX and OPEX estimates with evidence.
7. Multi-year financial model.
8. Funding plan and conditions.
9. Risk register.
10. Implementation schedule.
11. Evidence and assumptions register.
12. Documented go/no-go recommendation.

## 14. Evidence Standard

Classify every material statement as:
- Verified fact.
- Third-party estimate.
- Management assumption.
- Scenario assumption.
- Open question.

Record the source, date, responsible owner, and verification status.
The study must identify material information gaps explicitly.
