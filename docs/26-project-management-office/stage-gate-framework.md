# Stage-Gate Framework

## Purpose
Prevent premature commitments by requiring evidence and approval
before progressing to more costly or irreversible stages.

## Gate 0 — Concept and mandate
Evidence:
- Project objectives and intended beneficiaries.
- Initial scope and governance proposal.
- Known assumptions, constraints, and exclusions.

Decision: authorize further study only.

## Gate 1 — Feasibility and site validation
Evidence:
- Demand and stakeholder assessments.
- Site, utility, environmental, and hazard studies.
- Technical alternatives and lifecycle cost estimates.
- Legal and regulatory review.
- Initial implementation options.

Decision: proceed to detailed planning, revise, or stop.

## Gate 2 — Investment and funding readiness
Evidence:
- Reconciled CAPEX and OPEX model.
- Cash-flow and downside scenarios.
- Funding evidence and conditions.
- Operating model and accountable owners.
- Risk assessment and contingency rationale.

Decision: approve, defer, or reject a defined investment phase.

## Gate 3 — Procurement and delivery readiness
Evidence:
- Approved scope and budget.
- Technical specifications and acceptance criteria.
- Procurement plan and vendor due diligence.
- Required permits and contract approvals.
- Health, safety, security, and continuity plans.

Decision: authorize only the specifically approved procurement
or delivery package.

## Gate 4 — Testing and operational acceptance
Evidence:
- Completed inspections and testing.
- Defect and corrective-action register.
- Training, staffing, and maintenance readiness.
- Security, recovery, and service acceptance tests.
- Required licenses and operating approvals.

Decision: accept, conditionally accept, or reject the deliverable.

## Gate 5 — Operations and expansion
Evidence:
- Actual performance against approved KPIs.
- Verified demand and operating costs.
- Financial sustainability and lessons learned.
- Outstanding risks and remediation.

Decision: continue, improve, expand, or close.

## Gate authority
Each gate requires a named decision authority, documented evidence,
conflict-of-interest review where applicable, and a signed decision
record. A proposed gate is not an authorization by itself.
