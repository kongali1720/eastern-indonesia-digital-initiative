# Master Investment Budget
## Eastern Indonesia Digital Education & Data Center Initiative

**Budget envelope:** IDR 2,700,000,000,000
**Planning method:** Phased capital release against approved milestones
**Data center allocation:** IDR 1,100,000,000,000, included in the master total
**Status:** Preliminary planning framework — unverified allocations

---

## 1. Purpose and Budget Boundary

This document establishes a preliminary master budget envelope for the
proposed Eastern Indonesia Digital Education & Data Center Initiative.

The total planning envelope is IDR 2.7 trillion. The regional data center
allocation of IDR 1.1 trillion is included within this total.

The budget is a planning framework, not a verified cost estimate,
committed funding, approved expenditure, or investment offer.

Each allocation must be supported by a defined scope, cost estimate,
technical assessment, procurement evidence, legal review, and appropriate
approval before funds are committed or released.

## 3. Workstream Scope and Cost Breakdown

### 3.1 SMA IT Gowa — IDR 90 Billion Planning Allocation

Potential cost categories:
- Site screening and land-related costs, subject to legal due diligence.
- Concept design and professional services.
- Building and facilities.
- Classrooms, furniture, and learning equipment.
- Computer and science laboratories.
- Network and school IT.
- Recruitment, training, and pre-opening costs.
- Initial working capital, if explicitly included in the approved scope.

The IDR 90 billion allocation is not a verified project estimate. The
detailed scope must establish whether land, construction, equipment,
pre-opening expenditure, and working capital are included.

Target capacity: 540 students at maturity, subject to demand, site,
staffing, curriculum, and regulatory validation.

### 3.2 Digital Skills Course Institution — IDR 50 Billion

Potential cost categories:
- Training facilities and laboratory fit-out.
- Computers, networking equipment, and software.
- Course development and learning materials.
- Instructor recruitment and training.
- Learning platform and administration.
- Launch costs and working capital, if approved.

Required validation:
- Course-level demand and pricing.
- Cohort capacity and utilization.
- Instructor availability.
- Legal and regulatory requirements.
- Cost per learner and operating sustainability.

### 3.3 Future University Pathway — IDR 600 Billion

This is a long-term planning envelope, not an authorization to establish
or operate a university.

Potential categories to investigate:
- Higher-education feasibility and demand research.
- Academic program design and specialist advice.
- Site and facilities options.
- Faculty and leadership planning.
- Learning resources and laboratories.
- Technology and campus systems.
- Development and launch costs, if justified and approved.

No detailed construction allocation should be approved until the proposed
institution, programs, site, regulatory pathway, and implementation stages
have been evaluated.

### 3.4 Additional Education Facilities and Expansion Reserve — IDR 250 Billion

This allocation remains undefined pending a facilities masterplan.

Before approval:
- Identify the facilities and beneficiary workstreams.
- Confirm that the scope does not duplicate other allocations.
- Prepare concept designs and cost estimates.
- Document ownership, operational responsibility, and future OPEX.
- Approve the scope through formal change control.

### 3.5 Scholarships and Education Access — IDR 120 Billion

Potential uses:
- Need-based student scholarships.
- Merit-based awards where appropriate.
- Sponsored course seats.
- Approved learning-access programs.
- Student support linked to documented eligibility.

Before commitments are made, define the program duration, eligibility,
selection, renewal, funding source, restrictions, and reporting.

Distinguish a multi-year scholarship funding commitment from the amount
actually payable in any single year. Track restricted funds separately.

### 3.6 Shared Digital Platform and Education IT — IDR 100 Billion

Potential cost categories:
- Learning and student administration platforms.
- Network and identity management.
- Secure infrastructure and monitoring.
- Data integration and reporting.
- Backup, recovery, and continuity.
- Software implementation and support.
- Privacy and cybersecurity assessments.

The shared IT budget must exclude data center construction and equipment
already included in the separate data center workstream.

Kongali Server reference:
https://kongali1720.co.id/kongali-server/dashboard.html

This reference is not evidence of production readiness, availability,
security compliance, or suitability for student data.

### 3.7 Regional Data Center — IDR 1.1 Trillion

The data center is included in the IDR 2.7 trillion master envelope.

Its independent feasibility study should assess:
- Market demand and credible customer pipeline.
- Site, title, zoning, and environmental constraints.
- Electrical capacity, redundancy, and energy costs.
- Cooling and heat management.
- Fiber connectivity and carrier diversity.
- Building, racks, power distribution, and cabling.
- IT equipment and network systems.
- Physical security and fire protection.
- Monitoring, maintenance, staffing, and operations.
- Disaster recovery, security, and continuity.
- CAPEX, OPEX, funding, and sensitivity analysis.

The IDR 1.1 trillion amount is unverified. No procurement or construction
commitment should rely on this amount without a costed technical scope,
supplier quotations, customer-demand analysis, and formal approvals.

### 3.8 Risk and Contingency Reserve — IDR 330 Billion

The reserve is a planning allocation, not automatically available
expenditure.

Controls:
- Define eligible risks and use cases.
- Require documented justification and authorized approval.
- Track original allocation, approved use, and remaining balance.
- Prevent duplicate contingency within workstream estimates.
- Update the reserve as project risks and estimates change.
- Report material releases to authorized management and investors.

### 3.9 Feasibility, Legal, and Program Management — IDR 60 Billion

Potential work packages:
- Market and demand studies.
- Site and technical assessments.
- Legal and regulatory advice.
- Design and cost-estimation services.
- Financial modeling and independent review.
- Program management and reporting systems.
- Due diligence and procurement support.

The amount must be converted into costed work packages supported by
scopes of work, fee proposals, deliverables, and approval records.

## 4. Milestone-Based Funding Release

The following is a proposed governance structure for releasing the
master budget. Stage amounts are cumulative ceilings across workstreams;
they are not additional to the workstream allocations in Section 2.

### Gate 1 — Definition and Initial Feasibility
Authorize only approved studies, research, legal review, site screening,
technical assessments, and cost estimation.

Exit conditions:
- Approved project scopes.
- Responsible owners assigned.
- Evidence and assumptions register established.
- Study work plans and cost proposals approved.

### Gate 2 — Feasibility and Investment Readiness
Assess demand, regulatory feasibility, site options, technical requirements,
CAPEX, OPEX, cash flow, funding sources, and material risks.

Exit conditions:
- Feasibility reports completed to an agreed standard.
- Material cost assumptions supported by evidence.
- Downside scenarios and funding gaps documented.
- Separate investment decisions prepared for each workstream.

### Gate 3 — Investment and Procurement Approval
Approve workstream-specific budgets, funding conditions, procurement
strategies, contracts, and release schedules.

Exit conditions:
- Required approvals and conditions precedent satisfied.
- Funding availability documented.
- Scope and contract values reviewed.
- Reporting and spending controls operational.

### Gate 4 — Development
Release approved funds against contracts, verified progress, milestone
certificates, and documented payment requirements.

Exit conditions:
- Progress independently checked where appropriate.
- Budget variance and forecast-to-complete reviewed.
- Material changes formally approved.

### Gate 5 — Commissioning and Operational Readiness
Verify facilities, systems, safety, staffing, procedures, testing,
continuity, and applicable regulatory approvals.

Exit conditions:
- Operational acceptance documented.
- Outstanding critical defects resolved.
- Operating budgets and responsible teams established.

### Gate 6 — Operations and Performance Review
Monitor utilization, demand, service quality, revenue, operating costs,
cash flow, compliance, and risks.

Expansion or further capital deployment requires a separate approval
based on measured performance and updated feasibility.

## 5. Master Cash-Flow Model Requirements

The master cash-flow model must use monthly periods during development
and opening, followed by an appropriate operating forecast.

Required schedules:
1. Opening cash balance.
2. Funding receipts by source and date.
3. CAPEX payments by contract and milestone.
4. OPEX payments by workstream.
5. Tuition and course receipts based on collection assumptions.
6. Contracted customer receipts for the data center, if any.
7. Scholarship receipts and restricted expenditures.
8. Taxes and other relevant payments.
9. Debt service or investor distributions, only if legally agreed.
10. Closing cash balance.
11. Minimum liquidity and funding gap.
12. Contingency utilization and remaining reserve.

Do not treat investor interest, unsigned proposals, or unapproved grants
as received cash.

## 6. Required Financial Scenarios

Prepare conservative, base, and upside cases for each material workstream.

Stress tests should include:
- Lower enrollment and slower course utilization.
- Delayed opening.
- Construction and equipment cost increases.
- Lower tuition collection.
- Reduced scholarship sponsorship.
- Higher utilities and staffing costs.
- Data center delays, lower utilization, and higher energy costs.
- Delayed customer payments and higher working-capital needs.

The base case must be supported by the best available evidence.
The upside case must not be represented as guaranteed performance.

## 7. Accounting and Anti-Double-Counting Rules

- Maintain separate cost centers and reporting for each workstream.
- Define which entity owns each asset and signs each contract.
- Allocate shared IT and management costs transparently.
- Exclude data center construction and equipment from shared education IT
  if those costs are already included in the data center allocation.
- Avoid double-counting land, design, project management, contingency,
  and pre-opening costs.
- Reconcile budget, commitments, invoices, payments, and cash flow.
- Distinguish capital expenditure from operating expenditure under the
  applicable accounting policy.
- Maintain separate tracking for restricted scholarship funds.
- Do not use the master budget as evidence that funding is committed.

## 8. Investor Reporting

Monthly reporting should include:
- Original budget.
- Approved changes.
- Actual expenditure.
- Contractual commitments.
- Forecast to complete.
- Variance and explanation.
- Cash balance and projected runway.
- Funding received and outstanding commitments.
- Milestone status.
- Risks, incidents, and corrective actions.
- Contingency balance.

Quarterly reviews should reassess assumptions, cost estimates, financing,
delivery readiness, and the continued justification for each workstream.

## 9. Conditions Before External Investment Presentation

Before presenting this as a fully costed investment budget:
- Obtain site and technical information.
- Complete market-demand studies.
- Obtain dated vendor and professional-service quotations.
- Develop the detailed financial model.
- Confirm legal and regulatory pathways.
- Identify accountable project entities and owners.
- Validate funding sources and proposed investment terms.
- Obtain appropriate legal, accounting, engineering, and financial review.

## 10. Decision Rule

The IDR 2.7 trillion envelope is a strategic planning ceiling.
It does not require immediate spending of the full amount.

Capital should be released only for approved scopes, after required
evidence and conditions have been satisfied. A workstream may be
deferred, redesigned, or stopped if its feasibility cannot be demonstrated.
