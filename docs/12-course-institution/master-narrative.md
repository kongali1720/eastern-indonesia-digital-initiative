# Digital Skills Course and Training Institution

## 1. Vision

The proposed course institution will provide structured, practical
digital-skills training for school students, graduates, university
students, workers, entrepreneurs, and other eligible learners.

It may be developed as a complementary education workstream alongside
the SMA IT project. Its launch timing and legal structure must be
determined by feasibility, demand, staffing, and applicable licensing.

## 2. Strategic Objectives

- Expand access to practical technology education.
- Help learners build demonstrable project portfolios.
- Provide continuing education for different skill levels.
- Develop digital capabilities relevant to local businesses.
- Create a possible progression route from introductory learning
  to advanced technical study.
- Support responsible entrepreneurship and digital innovation.

The institution should not promise employment, income, or professional
certification unless a relevant outcome or credential is genuinely
available and properly authorized.

## 3. Proposed Course Portfolio

### A. Digital Foundations
- Computer operations and digital productivity.
- Internet safety and information literacy.
- Digital communication and collaboration.
- Introduction to data and spreadsheets.

### B. Programming and Software Development
- Programming fundamentals.
- Web development: HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
- Python fundamentals.
- Database and API fundamentals.
- Git, version control, and collaborative development.
- Software testing and deployment fundamentals.

### C. Networking and Cybersecurity
- Networking fundamentals.
- Operating systems and Linux administration.
- Defensive security principles.
- Secure configuration and access management.
- Security awareness and incident reporting.
- Authorized laboratory-based security exercises.

Cybersecurity training must be conducted in authorized environments.
Course participants must not be encouraged to access systems without
permission or collect other people's private information.

### D. Data and Artificial Intelligence
- Data analysis fundamentals.
- Data visualization.
- Responsible use of AI tools.
- Introduction to machine learning concepts.
- Data privacy, bias, and limitations of automated systems.

### E. Digital Business and Entrepreneurship
- Digital product planning.
- E-commerce fundamentals.
- Business process digitization.
- Digital marketing and analytics.
- Project management and business communication.
- Basic budgeting and financial literacy.

### F. Advanced and Specialist Programs
Advanced tracks should be launched only after demand, instructor
competence, lab capability, and operating costs have been validated.

## 4. Learner Segments

Potential segments include:
- SMA students.
- School graduates preparing for further study.
- University students seeking additional technical skills.
- Workers requiring digital upskilling.
- Local businesses seeking staff training.
- Community learners with limited access to digital education.

These segments are hypotheses for market research, not confirmed
customer demand.

## 5. Delivery Models

Possible delivery formats include:
- In-person classes.
- Scheduled laboratory sessions.
- Hybrid classes.
- Short workshops.
- Project-based bootcamps.
- Corporate or institutional training.
- Online learning supported by instructor guidance.

Each format must be evaluated for accessibility, learning outcomes,
instructor capacity, connectivity, and cost.

## 6. Curriculum and Assessment

Every course should specify:
- Target learner and prerequisites.
- Learning outcomes.
- Duration and learning hours.
- Lesson sequence and practical exercises.
- Instructor requirements.
- Equipment and software needs.
- Assessment rubric.
- Final project or demonstration.
- Attendance and completion rules.
- Certificate type and issuing authority, where applicable.

Certificates of course completion must not be represented as
government-recognized professional certifications unless that status
has been verified and authorization requirements are satisfied.

## 7. Business and Funding Model

Potential revenue sources, subject to legal review, include:
- Learner tuition.
- Corporate training contracts.
- Sponsored seats.
- Scholarships.
- Institutional partnerships.
- Approved grants or other permitted funding.

The financial plan must distinguish signed contracts, received
payments, expected sales, and uncommitted funding.

## 8. Staffing and Facilities

Potential roles include:
- Academic or training program lead.
- Qualified instructors.
- Learning support personnel.
- Laboratory and IT support.
- Admissions and learner administration.
- Business development and partner relations.

Facilities may include classrooms, computer laboratories, reliable
internet, software environments, and secure storage for equipment.

## 9. Quality Assurance

Track:
- Enrollment and attendance.
- Completion rates.
- Assessment results.
- Learner satisfaction.
- Portfolio completion.
- Repeat enrollment.
- Employer or partner feedback.
- Cost per learner.
- Scholarship participation and outcomes.

Use outcome data to revise the curriculum and discontinue programs
that fail to demonstrate demand or acceptable learning quality.

## 10. Regulatory Workstream

Before operating, verify the correct legal entity, business
classification, licensing route, course type, instructor requirements,
consumer disclosures, and certificate rules with the competent
authorities and qualified local advisers.

Do not assume that registration of a business entity alone authorizes
every education or certification activity.
