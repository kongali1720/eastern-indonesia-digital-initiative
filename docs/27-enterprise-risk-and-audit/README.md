# Enterprise Risk and Internal Audit

## Purpose
Provide a structured approach to identifying, evaluating, treating,
monitoring, and reporting risks across the initiative.

## Coverage
- Strategic and feasibility risk.
- Financial, liquidity, and funding risk.
- Construction, schedule, and cost-overrun risk.
- Procurement, vendor, and contract risk.
- Legal, regulatory, and permitting risk.
- Cybersecurity, privacy, and data risk.
- Education quality, safeguarding, and staffing risk.
- Energy, environmental, health, and safety risk.
- Fraud, conflicts of interest, and misuse-of-funds risk.
- Business continuity and operational resilience risk.

## Three distinct responsibilities
1. Workstream owners manage their risks and operate assigned controls.
2. Project governance reviews exposure, approves responses, and accepts
   residual risks within formally delegated authority.
3. Independent assurance evaluates whether controls are appropriately
   designed and operating effectively.

Internal audit should be independent of the activities it audits.
Where a dedicated audit function is not yet available, use qualified
independent external assurance and disclose the arrangement.

## Minimum risk-record fields
- Risk ID, description, cause, event, and consequence.
- Workstream and accountable risk owner.
- Inherent likelihood and impact.
- Existing controls and evidence.
- Residual likelihood and impact.
- Response, action owner, and due date.
- Early-warning indicator and escalation trigger.
- Review date, status, and acceptance authority.

## Risk appetite
Define approved tolerances before assigning formal risk acceptance.
High-impact risks, legal non-compliance, fraud allegations, serious
safety issues, and critical security incidents require escalation
under the relevant incident or governance procedure.

## Internal controls
Controls should protect assets, improve reporting reliability,
support compliance, and reduce the risk of unauthorized commitments.
