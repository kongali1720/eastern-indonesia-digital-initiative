# Audit and Assurance

## Purpose
Provide independent, evidence-based assessment of governance,
risk management, financial reporting, and operational controls.

## Independence
Auditors should have a defined mandate, unrestricted appropriate
access to evidence, confidentiality safeguards, and a reporting line
that protects independence.

Personnel should not independently audit work they designed,
approved, or operated. Disclose conflicts and arrange independent
review where needed.

## Proposed assurance plan
Prioritize reviews of:
1. Budget integrity and reconciliation.
2. Funding verification and restricted-fund tracking.
3. Procurement, vendor selection, and contract changes.
4. Payment authorization and bank reconciliation.
5. Project schedule and change-control discipline.
6. Cybersecurity and access controls.
7. Data-center readiness and continuity controls.
8. Scholarship selection and expenditure controls.
9. Regulatory, environmental, and safety obligations.

Timing and scope should be approved based on risk, project stage,
available evidence, and applicable professional standards.

## Audit finding fields
- Finding ID and review reference.
- Condition and supporting evidence.
- Applicable criterion or control objective.
- Cause and consequence.
- Risk rating and management response.
- Corrective action, owner, and due date.
- Independent validation and closure date.

## Finding lifecycle
Draft -> management response -> agreed action -> implementation
-> independent validation -> closed or formally escalated.

A management assertion alone does not establish that a finding is closed.
Closure requires sufficient supporting evidence.

## Reporting
Report material findings, overdue actions, repeated control failures,
and unresolved high risks to the authorized oversight body.

This framework is not a claim that an audit has been conducted or
that the project has been certified as compliant.
