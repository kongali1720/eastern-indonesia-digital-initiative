# Internal Controls

## Control objectives
Protect funds and assets, prevent unauthorized commitments, improve
reporting accuracy, support compliance, and preserve an audit trail.

## Core controls
### Budget and payments
- Maintain an approved budget and documented change process.
- Separate payment preparation, approval, and reconciliation.
- Verify supplier identity, contract, invoice, delivery, and bank details.
- Require documented approval for payment and changes in payee details.
- Reconcile bank accounts and project expenditure regularly.

### Procurement and conflicts of interest
- Apply documented evaluation criteria and approval thresholds.
- Record bids, scoring, selection rationale, and contract variations.
- Obtain conflict-of-interest declarations from relevant participants.
- Restrict exceptions to authorized, documented circumstances.

### Access and information
- Apply least-privilege access and periodic access reviews.
- Protect confidential investor, student, employee, and supplier data.
- Keep logs for important financial and administrative actions.
- Use approved retention, backup, and incident-response procedures.

### Reporting
- Reconcile financial reports to source records.
- Separate actuals, commitments, forecasts, and budget.
- Retain evidence for material assumptions and decisions.
- Investigate unexplained variances and unusual transactions.

## Control register fields
Identify the risk addressed, control objective, control owner,
frequency, evidence, reviewer, exception process, and test result.

## Limitations
A control design does not prove that the control is operating.
Controls require implementation, testing, remediation, and review.
