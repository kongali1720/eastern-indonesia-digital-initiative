# Risk Management Framework

## Assessment method
Assess each risk using documented likelihood and impact criteria.
Use a 1–5 scale as a preliminary working method:

Likelihood:
1 Rare
2 Unlikely
3 Possible
4 Likely
5 Almost certain

Impact:
1 Insignificant
2 Minor
3 Moderate
4 Major
5 Severe

Preliminary score = likelihood × impact.

This score is a prioritization aid, not a substitute for expert
judgment or mandatory legal, safety, or security escalation.

## Initial treatment bands
- 1–4: routine monitoring, subject to review of consequences.
- 5–9: assign response and review date.
- 10–16: management attention and documented mitigation plan.
- 17–25: prompt escalation to the authorized governance body.

Any serious safety, fraud, legal, privacy, or cybersecurity event
may require immediate escalation regardless of numeric score.

## Response options
- Avoid: change the plan to eliminate the exposure.
- Mitigate: reduce likelihood or impact.
- Transfer or share: use contracts or insurance where appropriate.
- Accept: document rationale and obtain authorized acceptance.
- Escalate: refer risks outside the owner's authority.

## Monitoring
Review risk ratings when:
- Scope, cost, schedule, site, or technology changes.
- Funding or critical dependencies change.
- A warning indicator is triggered.
- An incident, near miss, or audit finding occurs.
- New evidence materially changes the assessment.

## Risk ownership
Each risk must have one accountable owner. Action owners may differ.
The owner must maintain evidence, report changes, and verify closure.

## Reporting
Monthly reporting should include:
- Highest-rated risks and changes in exposure.
- Overdue mitigation actions.
- Risks beyond tolerance.
- Accepted residual risks and approver.
- New incidents and lessons learned.
- Dependencies requiring executive decisions.
