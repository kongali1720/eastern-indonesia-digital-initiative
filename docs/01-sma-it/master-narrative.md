# SMA IT Gowa — Master Project Narrative

## 1. Executive Vision

The Eastern Indonesia Digital Education Initiative proposes the
development of an information-technology-oriented senior secondary
school in Gowa, South Sulawesi, Indonesia.

The project aims to combine strong general education, technology
literacy, character development, entrepreneurship, and practical
problem-solving. Its long-term objective is to help students become
academically capable, digitally literate, and prepared for higher
education or employment.

The project will be developed in stages. The first priority is to
validate demand, establish a sustainable operating model, secure the
appropriate approvals, and prepare the school for responsible operation.

## 2. Strategic Rationale

Digital skills are increasingly relevant across education, business,
public services, manufacturing, agriculture, finance, and creative
industries. A school with a carefully designed technology-oriented
learning environment can provide students with earlier exposure to
computing, programming, data literacy, and responsible technology use.

The project must not assume that a technology-oriented school will
automatically attract sufficient enrollment. Demand, affordability,
existing school alternatives, family expectations, and local employment
pathways must be examined through field research.

## 3. Target Capacity

The preliminary planning target is 540 students at maturity:

- Three grade levels: X, XI, and XII.
- Six classes per grade.
- Thirty students per class.
- Eighteen classes in total.

This is an illustrative capacity model, not a confirmed enrollment
forecast or a regulatory determination.

A phased enrollment model should be assessed against the school's
readiness, recruitment pipeline, teacher availability, facilities,
and operating cash flow.

## 4. Educational Model

The proposed learning model combines:

1. Required national curriculum subjects.
2. Mathematics, sciences, languages, and social sciences.
3. Computer science and programming fundamentals.
4. Digital literacy, data literacy, and responsible AI awareness.
5. Networking and introductory cybersecurity concepts.
6. Project-based learning and collaborative problem-solving.
7. Entrepreneurship, communication, and financial literacy.
8. Character education, citizenship, and student wellbeing.

The final curriculum must be reviewed by qualified education
professionals and aligned with applicable Indonesian requirements.

## 5. Facilities and Learning Environment

Facilities should be developed according to verified student capacity,
curriculum needs, safety requirements, and available funding.

Potential facilities include:
- General classrooms.
- Computer laboratories.
- Science learning facilities.
- Library and collaborative learning areas.
- Teacher preparation and administration rooms.
- Student counselling and wellbeing facilities.
- Accessible sanitation and safe circulation areas.
- Reliable internet connectivity and managed school networks.
- Backup, maintenance, and physical security provisions.

A detailed space program and construction estimate must follow a site
assessment and concept design.

## 6. Students, Admissions, and Scholarships

The proposed model may combine fee-paying students with scholarship
recipients.

Admissions must use transparent and published criteria. Scholarship
programs should define eligibility, selection, award value, duration,
renewal, funding source, and reporting responsibilities.

Scholarship funding must be tracked separately where required by donor
or agreement conditions. Uncommitted donations must not be treated as
available cash.

## 7. Human Resources

The school will require qualified academic leadership, teachers,
administrative personnel, IT support, student support staff, and
facilities operations personnel.

The staffing plan must include:
- Required qualifications and applicable checks.
- Recruitment and onboarding costs.
- Salary and benefits assumptions.
- Professional development.
- Performance evaluation.
- Student safeguarding responsibilities.
- Substitution and succession arrangements.

## 8. Technology and Data Protection

Technology should support learning rather than replace sound teaching.

The school may evaluate learning management, student administration,
attendance, digital libraries, managed devices, identity management,
backup, and reporting systems.

The Kongali Server dashboard is a reference for infrastructure
exploration, not proof of production readiness or regulatory compliance:
https://kongali1720.co.id/kongali-server/dashboard.html

Before deployment, conduct architecture, security, availability,
privacy, backup, recovery, and operational reviews. Use synthetic
student data during development and restrict access to real records.

## 9. Operating Sustainability

The school must prepare a multi-year operating model covering:
- Tuition and other permitted revenue.
- Scholarship sponsorship and restrictions.
- Salaries and benefits.
- Utilities, connectivity, and maintenance.
- Teaching materials and student activities.
- Insurance, administration, and compliance.
- IT systems and cybersecurity.
- Working capital and contingency.

The financial model must include downside scenarios for slower
enrollment, lower affordability, delayed opening, and cost increases.

## 10. Implementation Gates

Gate 1: Concept and governance approval.
Gate 2: Market and demand validation.
Gate 3: Site, legal, and regulatory due diligence.
Gate 4: Financial feasibility and documented funding.
Gate 5: Design, procurement, staffing, and construction readiness.
Gate 6: Operational readiness and applicable approvals.
Gate 7: Opening, monitoring, and continuous improvement.

## 11. Intended Outcomes

Potential outcomes include improved digital literacy, stronger
student project portfolios, progression to further education,
responsible technology use, and greater access through scholarships.

Targets must be measurable, realistic, and supported by baseline data.

## 12. Important Qualification

This document describes a proposed project. It does not confirm land
ownership, licensing, investor commitments, construction contracts,
student demand, or final project costs.
