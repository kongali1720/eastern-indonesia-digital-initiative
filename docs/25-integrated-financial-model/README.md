# Integrated Financial Model

## 1. Purpose

Provide a transparent financial planning framework for the Eastern
Indonesia Digital Education & Data Center Initiative in Gowa,
South Sulawesi.

This framework connects capital expenditure (CAPEX), operating
expenditure (OPEX), funding sources, cash flow, financial risks,
and implementation decisions.

This is a planning model, not an audited forecast, investment offer,
funding commitment, or assurance of financial returns.

## 3. Model architecture

The model should connect the following schedules:

1. Master investment budget and CAPEX phasing.
2. Operating costs by facility and workstream.
3. Revenue and other operating income, where evidenced.
4. Funding sources, restrictions, timing, and conditions.
5. Annual and monthly cash flow as project detail becomes available.
6. Base, downside, and upside scenarios.
7. Risk, contingency, and sensitivity analysis.
8. Management KPIs and decision gates.

## 4. CAPEX categories

Capture costs by workstream, phase, and asset category, including:

- Land, site investigation, and site preparation.
- Buildings, classrooms, laboratories, and campus facilities.
- Data-center buildings, IT equipment, cooling, and power systems.
- Network and cybersecurity infrastructure.
- Renewable energy and utility systems.
- Education technology, software, and digital platforms.
- Professional services, permitting, and project management.
- Contingency and lifecycle replacement planning.

Avoid counting the same item in both a workstream budget and a
central contingency or shared-infrastructure budget.

Land acquisition, taxes, financing costs, escalation, and replacement
reserves must be explicitly included or excluded after qualified review.

## 5. OPEX categories

Model recurring costs separately from capital expenditure:

- Staff, teachers, technical specialists, and administration.
- Electricity, water, cooling, connectivity, and utilities.
- Software subscriptions, cloud services, and cybersecurity.
- Data-center operations, maintenance, and equipment support.
- Scholarships, training delivery, and student services.
- Insurance, compliance, audit, and professional services.
- Repairs, consumables, waste management, and security.
- Equipment replacement and lifecycle maintenance.

Document the basis of each estimate, such as vendor quotations,
staffing plans, benchmark studies, or an explicitly labeled assumption.

## 6. Revenue and operating income

Do not assume revenue merely because a facility is proposed.

Potential sources requiring validation include:
- Tuition and training fees.
- Data-center hosting, colocation, or managed services.
- Cloud and digital services.
- Research, incubation, and industry service contracts.
- Grants, sponsorships, and donations.

Record capacity, utilization, pricing, customer evidence, collection
timing, tax treatment, and operating costs for each applicable source.

Grants, donations, equity, and debt are funding sources, not automatically
operating revenue. Debt and equity must be modeled separately.

## 7. Funding schedule

Track each source by:
- Source and responsible counterparty.
- Amount proposed, committed, received, and remaining.
- Evidence and date of verification.
- Conditions precedent and permitted use.
- Expected drawdown date and restrictions.
- Repayment, interest, ownership, or reporting obligations.

Only legally documented and appropriately verified commitments should
be treated as committed funding. A discussion with a potential investor
is not a funding commitment.

## 8. Cash flow

For each period calculate:

Opening cash
+ verified funding received
+ operating cash receipts
- CAPEX paid
- OPEX paid
- debt service and financing costs
= closing cash

Separate operating, investing, and financing cash flows. Avoid
double-counting transfers between project accounts.

Model timing explicitly: a budget allocation is not necessarily cash
paid in the same period.

## 9. Scenario analysis

Maintain three scenarios:

- Downside: delayed approvals, higher costs, slower utilization,
  weaker collections, or delayed funding.
- Base: validated planning assumptions and the approved implementation
  schedule.
- Upside: stronger demand or operating efficiency supported by evidence.

Do not label a scenario as a forecast until its inputs and basis have
been reviewed.

## 10. Core metrics

Calculate when supporting inputs are available:
- Total estimated project cost and variance to budget.
- Funding coverage and unfunded capital requirement.
- Annual CAPEX and OPEX.
- Closing cash and minimum liquidity.
- Operating surplus or deficit by workstream.
- Data-center utilization and revenue per occupied unit.
- Cash runway, where meaningful.
- NPV, IRR, and debt-service coverage only when the required
  timing, cash flow, financing, and discount-rate assumptions exist.

A positive accounting result does not necessarily mean positive cash flow.

## 11. Controls and approval gates

1. Each estimate has an owner, source, date, and confidence level.
2. Finance reconciles all workstream budgets to the master budget.
3. Procurement quotations are distinguished from approved contracts.
4. Funding is distinguished as proposed, committed, or received.
5. Budget changes require documented approval.
6. Forecasts are updated against actual results.
7. Material variances trigger review and corrective actions.
8. Independent professional review is required before investor reliance.

## 12. Important limitations

The current model is a framework pending verified project inputs.
It does not establish project feasibility, funding availability,
investment returns, land ownership, permits, or construction approval.

The IDR 2.7 trillion envelope and IDR 1.1 trillion data-center
allocation remain preliminary planning assumptions.
